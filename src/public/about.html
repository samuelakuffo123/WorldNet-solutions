Frequently asked questions

Practical answers about our networks, security, clients and how we get started.

Networks & fiber

What kinds of networks do you build?

We design and implement LAN, WAN, MAN and enterprise networks over radio and fiber, with structured cabling in UTP, STP and fiber — across offices, campuses, banks, ISPs and long-haul corridors like the Eastern Corridor fiber project from Accra to Bawku.

Can you support campus backbone upgrades?

Yes. We have delivered backbone links and campus networks (for example KNUST Sunyani campus) using phased migration plans that minimize disruption while expanding capacity and resilience.

Security & support

What security services do you provide?

Systems security, anti-virus support, firewall and VPN configuration (SonicWall, Cyberoam, Smoothwall), plus physical security & surveillance for offices and campuses.

Do you offer maintenance contracts?

Yes. We provide servicing & maintenance contracts for computers and peripherals, field and onsite engineering, remote support, and printer, monitor and UPS repairs.

Services & clients

Who do you count among your clients?

Banks including GCB, Ecobank, Cal Bank, TTB and Bank of Ghana; ISPs like BusyInternet; and public institutions from the Council of State to Ghana Customs (CEPS) — as well as long-haul fiber consultancy for Alcatel-Lucent.

What makes WorldNet different?

We are a group of specialized ICT companies working as one team — cabling, power systems, network security and digital TV — with certified staff carrying more than 150 years of cumulative experience and a national-scale track record.

Getting started & coverage

How do I start a project with you?

Book a free consultation. We start with a site or network audit, design a phased plan with a transparent custom quote, then deliver and verify before moving into support and maintenance.

Do you support clients outside Accra?

Yes. Our field and onsite engineers work across Ghana — from institutional networks in Kumasi and Sunyani to long-haul fiber along the Eastern Corridor — backed by 24/7 remote support.
